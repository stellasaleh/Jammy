Three things no other app does

Jammy is built for people who play, and it shows in the parts that matter.

01

Distance you set

Three miles, seven, fifteen, or anywhere. A bandmate forty miles away isn't a bandmate — they're a nice idea. Distance is measured from where you're standing, not where you signed up.

02

Complementary, not similar

Tell Jammy you play trumpet and it stops showing you other trumpet players. It shows you the drummer who's been looking for horns. A band is different parts, not matching ones.

03

Hear them first

Every profile carries recordings, and you can send thirty seconds of yourself playing straight into a message. No more arranging a rehearsal that was never going to work.

Then it keeps the band together

Bands rarely break up over music. They break up over logistics — whose turn it is to book the room, what time doors are, who said they'd bring the PA.

  • Every band gets a page with the roster, what everyone plays, and the full schedule.
  • Practices stay private. Only your band sees the time and the address — a rehearsal room is often someone's home.
  • Shows are public, so people can actually come.
  • Reminders that mean something. Not "event at 9pm" — "you promised to bring the PA."

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes, and there's no plan to change that. No subscription, no premium tier, no paywall on messaging. Venues and music businesses pay for promoted listings, and that's what funds it.

Is this a dating app?

No. It borrows the swipe because it's a good way to go through a lot of profiles quickly, and nothing else. Jammy matches people on complementary instruments and rehearsal schedules. Romantic approaches are against the rules and get accounts removed.

Do I have to be good?

No. There's no skill rating and no leaderboard of players. Plenty of people on Jammy are looking for someone to learn alongside, and the profile recordings exist so both of you know what you're getting before you spend a Tuesday evening on it.

Can I use it if I don't want to join a band?

Yes. Session players, co-writers, people who want one-off jams, producers looking for vocalists, and bands looking for a dep for one gig all use the same tools.

Where does it work?

Boston first, deliberately. An app like this is only useful when there are enough musicians in a few square miles — a hundred people in one neighbourhood beats a thousand spread across the country. More cities follow once Boston works.

What do you do with my location?

Jammy stores it rounded to about a mile and never shows anyone your coordinates — other musicians only ever see how far away you are. Rehearsal addresses are visible only to your own bandmates.

How old do I have to be?

18 or over. Jammy has private messaging and location-based discovery between strangers, so we keep it to adults.
