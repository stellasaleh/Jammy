Who sees it

Jammy is built for people who play — not a general music audience. Every account lists the instruments they play, the genres they play them in, and the neighbourhood they're in.

Actively looking

People on Jammy are searching for a drummer, a room to rehearse in, or a gig to play this month. That's the whole reason they opened the app.

Local by design

Musicians filter by distance — three, seven, fifteen miles. Your placement is shown to people in your city, not to someone two states away.

Targeted by instrument

Selling cymbals? Show it to drummers. Booking a jazz night? Reach jazz players. You pick who it goes to when you submit.

Rates

No contracts, no minimum spend, no auction. You pay for a run, we report what it did, and you decide whether to do it again.

Questions

How do I pay?

By invoice, once the placement is approved and scheduled — bank transfer or card. Nothing is taken at the point you submit this form, and you can pull out before it runs at no cost.

How do I know it worked?

At the end of the run you get a report: how many times it was shown, how many people tapped it, and the click rate. If a placement does badly we'll tell you, and we'd rather fix the copy than take money for something that isn't landing.

Can I change the wording after it's live?

Yes. Email us and we'll update it, usually the same day.

Will it look like a normal post?

No, deliberately. Every placement carries a coloured border and a PROMOTED label. Ads that disguise themselves as real posts wreck the trust that makes the app worth advertising on in the first place.

Do you sell my details to anyone?

No. We use your email to talk to you about your placement. That's it.

What if I'm not a venue or a shop?

Send the form anyway and explain in the last box. Festivals, teachers, luthiers, studios, promoters and community music programmes all have a reason to be in front of local musicians.
