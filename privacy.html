Privacy Policy

Last updated 12 November 2026

The short version

We collect what Jammy needs to work and nothing else. We do not sell your data, we do not share it with advertisers, and we never show anyone your exact location.

Other musicians see how far away you are, never where you are. Rehearsal addresses are visible only to your own bandmates. You can download everything we hold on you, or delete your account entirely, at any time, from inside the app.

This policy explains what Jammy collects, why, how long we keep it, and what rights you have. If anything here is unclear, email contact@jammy.social and we will answer in plain English.

Who we are

Jammy is operated by Jammy LLC, a Washington limited liability company. For any privacy question, or to exercise any right described below, contact contact@jammy.social. Our registered agent's address is on file with the Washington Secretary of State and is available on request.

Location, specifically

Jammy is built around distance, so location matters more here than in most apps, and so does handling it carefully.

  • We ask for location permission once. If you decline, Jammy falls back to an approximate city-level estimate, or you can pick your city manually. The app works either way.
  • We request coarse accuracy only, never precise positioning, and only while the app is in use. Never in the background.
  • Your coordinates are rounded to roughly one mile before they are stored, on your device, before they are sent anywhere.
  • Other users are shown a computed distance such as "3.1 miles away", and never your coordinates.
  • Rehearsal addresses attached to a band event are visible only to members of that band, and are never included in anything public.

What we never do

  • We do not sell your personal information to anyone, for any purpose.
  • We do not share your data with advertisers. Advertisers choose a city and a genre; they never receive a list of who saw their ad.
  • We do not use third-party tracking or advertising networks inside the app.
  • We do not read your private messages except when a message is reported to us, in which case a moderator reviews only what was reported.

Who can see what

  • Public to signed-in users: your name, handle, photo, bio, instruments, genres, public recordings, posts, and your approximate distance from them.
  • Your band only: practice times and addresses, private recordings, band notes, and who agreed to bring what.
  • Only the recipient: direct messages and jam clips you send.
  • Only us: your email or phone, date of birth, and device information.

How long we keep it

  • While your account is open: your profile, posts, bands and messages.
  • After you delete your account: your profile and content are removed within 30 days. Messages you sent may remain visible to the person who received them, in the same way a sent text does.
  • Reports and safety records are kept for up to 12 months after they are resolved, so repeat behaviour can be identified.
  • Technical logs are kept for up to 90 days.

Your rights

Wherever you live, Jammy gives every user the same rights:

  • See your data. Settings, then Account and safety, then Download my data.
  • Correct it. Edit your profile at any time.
  • Delete it. Settings, then Delete account. Permanent, and not hidden behind an email request.
  • Withdraw permissions. Turn off location or notifications in your phone settings; Jammy keeps working.
  • Object or complain. Email us. If you are in the EU or UK you may also complain to your local data protection authority.

If you are a California resident you have the rights described above under the CCPA, including the right not to be discriminated against for exercising them. We do not sell personal information, so there is nothing to opt out of.

Children

Jammy is 18 and over. We ask for date of birth at signup and reject accounts below that age. We do not knowingly collect information from anyone under 18. If you believe a minor is using Jammy, email us and we will remove the account.

Who we share data with

Only the services needed to run the app, and only what they need:

  • Supabase for database, authentication and file storage.
  • Firebase Cloud Messaging for delivering push notifications.
  • An automated content scanning service for checking uploaded images and audio.
  • Apple and Google for app distribution and crash reporting.

We may also disclose information if legally required to, or where necessary to protect someone from harm.

Security

Data is encrypted in transit and at rest. Access is controlled by row-level security rules, meaning private messages and band schedules are inaccessible even to someone holding the app public key.

Uploaded files fall into two groups, and we want to be precise about the difference. Audio clips sent inside a conversation are private: they are served through short-lived signed links, and only the people in that conversation can open them. Profile photos, post images and tracks you add to the radio are public by design — they are meant to be seen — and anyone who has the file's web address can open it, including someone who is not signed in to Jammy. Treat anything you put on your profile or in a post as public. You can remove a file at any time, and we delete it when you do.

No system is perfect. If we ever discover a breach affecting your data, we will tell you.

Changes

If we change this policy in a way that materially affects you, we will notify you in the app before the change takes effect.

Before you publish this

This is a careful draft, not legal advice. It names Jammy LLC as the operator — do not publish it until that company actually exists, because naming an entity that hasn't been formed is itself a misrepresentation. Until then, either hold the page back or name yourself as the operator.

Have it read by a lawyer or a free law school clinic, and check every statement is still true of the app you ship. A privacy policy that overpromises is worse than none at all.
