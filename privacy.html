Privacy Policy

Last updated 24 November 2026

The short version

We collect what Jammy needs to work and nothing else. We do not sell your data and we do not give it to advertisers.

Nobody on Jammy can see your date of birth or your coordinates — not other members, not through our app, and not by querying our database directly. Other musicians are shown how far away you are, never where you are. Rehearsal addresses are visible only to your own bandmates.

You can download everything we hold about you, or delete your account for good, from inside the app. Neither is hidden behind an email request.

This policy describes what Jammy actually does today, not what it is planned to do. Where a thing is not built yet, it says so. If anything here is unclear, email contact@jammy.social and we will answer in plain English.

Who we are

Jammy is operated by Jammy LLC, a limited liability company registered in Washington State, United States. Our registered agent's address is on file with the Washington Secretary of State and is available on request.

For any privacy question, or to exercise any right described below, contact contact@jammy.social.

What we do not collect

These are worth naming, because many apps like this one do collect them:

  • No precise location. We ask your device for coarse accuracy only, and only while you are using the app — never in the background.
  • No contacts, no calendar, no photo library. We only ever receive a file you choose to upload.
  • No advertising or analytics trackers. There is no Google Analytics, no Meta pixel, no advertising SDK anywhere in Jammy.
  • No push notifications, and no device tokens. Jammy does not send push notifications at all yet, so nothing identifying your device is stored.
  • No automated scanning of what you upload. We want to be straight about this one: your photos and recordings are not checked by any automated system. Uploads are reviewed only when somebody reports them.

Location, specifically

Jammy is built around distance, so location matters more here than in most apps, and so does handling it carefully.

  • We ask for location permission once. If you say no, you pick your city by hand instead, and the app works either way.
  • We request coarse accuracy only, never precise positioning, and only while the app is open.
  • Your coordinates are rounded to roughly one mile on your own device, before they are sent to us. We never receive a precise position, so we could not reveal one even if we were compelled to.
  • Other members are shown a computed distance such as "3.1 miles away". The stored coordinate itself is readable by nobody — not other members, and not by any query a member could send to our database.
  • Rehearsal addresses attached to a band event are visible only to the members of that band, and are never included in anything public. A practice can never be made public, because the address is usually somebody's home.

Who can see what

  • Any signed-in member: your name, handle, photo, bio, city, instruments, genres, your public recordings and posts, and your approximate distance from them.
  • Your band only: practice times and addresses, band recordings, band notes, and who agreed to bring what.
  • Only the people in the conversation: direct messages, and audio clips sent inside a conversation.
  • Only you: your date of birth and your stored coordinates.
  • Only us: your email address or phone number, and reports you have made.

These are enforced by the database itself, row by row and column by column, rather than by the app being polite. A member who went around our app and queried our database directly would get exactly the same answers.

Public files, and files that are not

Uploaded files fall into two groups and the difference matters.

  • Audio clips sent inside a conversation are private. They are served through short-lived signed links, and only the people in that conversation can open them.
  • Profile photos, post images and tracks you put on the radio are public by design. Anyone who has the file's web address can open it, including someone who is not signed in to Jammy. Treat anything you put on your profile or in a post as public.

You can remove any file at any time, and we delete it from storage when you do.

What we never do

  • We do not sell your personal information to anyone, for any purpose.
  • We do not give your data to advertisers. An advertiser chooses a city and a genre. They never receive a list of who saw their ad, and they have no way to query for one.
  • We do not read your private messages, except when a message is reported to us, in which case we look at what was reported and nothing else.

We do keep a record of which ads were shown and clicked, including which account they were shown to, so that we can tell an advertiser how many people saw their ad. That record stays with us.

Who else is involved

Running Jammy means a small number of other companies handle some of your data. These are all of them:

  • Supabase — our database, sign-in and file storage. Everything described above is stored there.
  • GitHub Pages — serves the app itself. Like any web host, it sees the internet address of visitors.
  • Google Fonts — Jammy loads two typefaces from Google's font service, which means Google receives your internet address when a page loads. No account information is sent, and we do not use any other Google service.

We may also disclose information if we are legally required to, or where it is necessary to protect someone from harm.

How long we keep it

  • While your account is open: your profile, posts, recordings, bands and messages.
  • When you delete your account: your profile and everything attached to it is deleted immediately, not queued for later. Messages you already sent remain visible to the people who received them, in the same way a sent text does.
  • Reports: when the account involved is deleted, the report is kept but detached from the person, so a resolved safety matter does not vanish and a deleted person is not still named in it.
  • Server logs: our hosting providers keep their own standard access logs. We do not run separate logging of our own, and we do not control how long theirs are retained.

Your rights

Wherever you live, Jammy gives every member the same rights:

  • See your data. Settings, then Account and safety, then Download my data. It includes your date of birth and your rounded coordinates, because they are yours.
  • Correct it. Edit your profile at any time. Your date of birth is the exception: it is fixed at signup, so that an account cannot change its age later.
  • Delete it. Settings, then Delete account. Permanent, immediate, and not hidden behind an email request.
  • Withdraw permissions. Turn off location in your phone settings. Jammy keeps working.
  • Object or complain. Email us. If you are in the EU or UK you may also complain to your local data protection authority.

If you are a California resident, you have these rights under the CCPA, including the right not to be treated differently for using them. We are far below the size at which that law applies to a business, so we are giving you these rights because we think you should have them, not because we are required to.

Children

Jammy is 18 and over. We ask for your date of birth at signup, and the database itself refuses to create an account for anyone younger — it is a rule in the data, not a check that can be skipped. We do not knowingly collect information from anyone under 18. If you believe a minor is using Jammy, email us and we will remove the account.

We are honest about the limit of this: a date of birth typed into a box is not proof of age, and we do not currently verify it further.

Security

Data is encrypted in transit and at rest. Access is controlled by rules held in the database — which rows you may read, and which columns — so private messages, band schedules, dates of birth and stored coordinates are inaccessible to other members no matter how they ask.

No system is perfect, and Jammy is a young app built by one person. If we ever discover a breach affecting your data, we will tell you.

Changes

If we change this policy in a way that materially affects you, we will tell you in the app before the change takes effect. If we build something new — push notifications, automated scanning of uploads, anything that collects more than is listed above — this page will say so before it ships, not after.
